Handover and snagging: taking the keys to a new Dubai home

The completion certificate, your title deed, the inspection before you accept, and the years the developer stays responsible for defects.

Off-plan · Updated 16 September 2026 · 7 sources

In short

  • When a project receives its completion certificate, the developer must register each sold unit in the name of the buyer who has met their obligations. Your Oqood registration becomes a title deed.
  • Snagging is the inspection you carry out before you accept the home. You list every defect and every piece of incomplete work for the developer to put right.
  • Article 40 of Dubai Law No. 6 of 2019 makes the developer liable for defective installations for one year from handover and for structural defects for ten years from the completion certificate.
  • Any clause that cuts those liabilities short is void.

The completion certificate and your deed

A building is complete when its planning authority says so by issuing a completion certificate. Depending on the area that authority is the Dubai Development Authority, Trakhees or Dubai Municipality. Until the certificate exists the developer cannot hand over.

Once it exists, Article 8 of Law No. 13 of 2008 requires the developer to enter the project in the property register and to register each sold unit in the name of the buyer who has met their obligations. That is the moment your Oqood registration gives way to a title deed. DLD charges a fee to issue the deed; ask the developer for the amount. If you still owe an instalment, settle it first, because the registration waits for buyers who have paid.

Before handover day

  • Read the handover notice for what the developer asks you to pay: the final instalment, any administration charge, and the first service charge period
  • Check the service charge against what the sale agreement said, and ask for the RERA-approved budget
  • Confirm the DEWA connection process and whether a deposit is collected at handover
  • Book a snagging inspection for a date before you sign the handover documents, not after

The developer's handover team will have an appointment system and a document list, usually your identification and proof that the final payment has cleared. Ask for the list in advance so the appointment is not wasted, and ask whether the keys are released on the day or after the deed is issued.

The snagging inspection

Snagging is market practice rather than a legal step, so nothing in the law forces the developer to wait for it. The developer's handover documents usually include an acceptance form, and once you sign it, the developer can treat the home as accepted. Inspect first. Walk every room with a checklist, or hire an inspector who does this for a living, and hand the developer a written, dated list with photographs.

  1. Finishes.Walls, ceilings, floors, tiling, joinery, doors and windows. Look for cracks, chips, gaps, scratches and uneven lines.
  2. Installations.Air conditioning, electrics, every socket and switch, water pressure, hot water, drainage and extraction, each tested while running.
  3. Kitchen and bathrooms.Appliances, silicone and grout, taps and shower screens, waterproofing under and around wet areas.
  4. Against the contract.Layout, size and specification checked against the sale and purchase agreement and the brochure you bought from.
  5. Outside.Balcony drainage, railings, external doors and, on a villa, the boundary wall, garden drainage and the roof.

Agree a date for the developer to clear the list and a second visit to confirm it. Keep copies of everything. The one-year installation liability starts at handover, so it has already started once you take the keys.

How long the developer is liable

DefectDeveloper liable forCounted from
Defective installations: mechanical, electrical, sanitary and sewerage1 yearHandover of the unit, or the completion certificate if the owner refuses to take possession
Defects in the structural parts of the building10 yearsThe completion certificate

Both periods come from Article 40 of Dubai Law No. 6 of 2019, and the same article makes any agreement to the contrary void. A clause in your sale agreement that shortens them has no effect. Separately, the UAE's Civil Transactions Law covers contractors and engineers. Federal Decree-Law No. 25 of 2025 holds them jointly liable for ten years for collapse or for defects that threaten a building's stability or safety, in Articles 821 to 824, in force from 1 June 2026.

Report defects in writing as soon as you find them, with dates, so that a claim inside the period is easy to prove.

Delays, defaults and cancelled projects

If the delay is yours, Law No. 13 of 2008 as amended by Law No. 19 of 2020 applies. After a 30-day notice served through DLD, the developer may terminate. It may keep up to 40% of the unit's value where the project is more than 60% complete, or up to 25% below that. It must refund the rest within a year, or within 60 days of reselling the unit, whichever comes first.

If the delay is the developer's, the sale and purchase agreement governs. Read the clause on late completion before you sign, because it sets whatever compensation you are entitled to. If a project never starts for reasons outside the developer's control, or RERA cancels it, the developer must refund buyers' payments under the escrow law.

Selling before the keys

You can resell an off-plan home before completion. Under Article 3 of Law No. 13 of 2008 every such sale must be entered in the interim register, or it is void. The developer sets its own conditions for the resale NOC, written into the sale and purchase agreement, including how much of the price must already be paid. Selling property in Dubai covers the steps.

Ask the developer for its consent conditions and any administration fee in writing before you agree a price with a buyer. No deed changes hands; the Oqood record is updated to the buyer's name, and the deed is issued to them at completion.

Questions buyers ask

When does an off-plan buyer get the title deed?

After the project's completion certificate is issued. Article 8 of Law No. 13 of 2008 then requires the developer to register each sold unit in the name of the buyer who has met their obligations. DLD charges a fee to issue the deed; the developer can tell you the amount.

What is snagging?

The inspection of a new home before you accept it. You list defects and incomplete work for the developer to put right. It is market practice, not a legal procedure, so arrange it before you sign the handover documents.

How long must a developer fix defects in Dubai?

One year from handover for defective mechanical, electrical, sanitary and sewerage installations, and ten years from the completion certificate for structural defects, under Article 40 of Dubai Law No. 6 of 2019. Any agreement to the contrary is void.

Who issues the completion certificate?

The planning authority for the area: the Dubai Development Authority, Trakhees or Dubai Municipality.

What happens if I stop paying before handover?

After a 30-day notice through DLD the developer may terminate and keep up to 40% of the unit's value on a project more than 60% complete, or up to 25% below that, refunding the balance within a year or 60 days of resale, under Law No. 13 of 2008 as amended by Law No. 19 of 2020.

What if the developer is late?

Your sale and purchase agreement sets your entitlement, so read the late completion clause before you sign. If the project never starts or RERA cancels it, the escrow law requires your payments to be refunded.

Can I sell an off-plan home before handover?

Yes, subject to the developer's conditions and NOC. Under Article 3 of Law No. 13 of 2008 the sale must be entered in DLD's interim register or it is void.

Buying a home in Dubai: the process from start to keys

Who can own, what to budget, which forms you sign, and what happens at the registration trustee when the home becomes yours.

Buying · Updated 15 September 2026 · 9 sources

In short

  • Anyone can buy freehold property in Dubai's designated freehold areas, whatever their nationality, and a UAE residence visa is not required.
  • Plan for about 7% on top of the price of a ready home. That covers the 4% DLD transfer fee, the customary 2% agency fee plus VAT, and the trustee, title deed and NOC fees.
  • You and the seller sign Form F, the DLD's standard sale contract, and by custom you pay a 10% deposit at that point.
  • The home becomes yours at a DLD registration trustee office, where the seller is paid and your title deed is issued.

Who can buy property in Dubai

Dubai lets buyers of every nationality own property outright in its designated freehold areas. Regulation No. 3 of 2006 listed the first of these areas, and the list has grown since. Dubai Marina, Downtown Dubai, Palm Jumeirah, Business Bay, Dubai Hills Estate, Jumeirah Village Circle and Dubai Creek Harbour are all on it. Our area guides cover each of them.

Under Dubai Law No. 7 of 2006, a non-UAE national may hold freehold title in a designated area with no time limit, or a leasehold right of up to 99 years. Outside the designated areas the rules differ, so confirm that an area is freehold before you make an offer.

You do not have to live in the UAE. A non-resident buyer identifies themselves with a valid passport at the registration trustee. If you cannot attend in person, an attorney can sign for you under a power of attorney. One issued abroad must be notarised, legalised by the UAE embassy or consulate, attested by the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs, translated into Arabic and less than two years old. Buying property in Dubai from abroad walks through it.

Setting a budget that includes the fees

The price is not the whole number. On a ready home, the fees come to about 7% of the price. Fix your budget with those fees inside it from the start.

  • DLD transfer fee: 4% of the price, paid by the buyer by custom
  • Title deed fees: AED 520 for an apartment or villa
  • Registration trustee fee: AED 4,000 plus VAT, or AED 2,000 plus VAT when the price is under AED 500,000
  • Agency fee: usually 2% of the price plus VAT
  • Developer NOC: AED 500 to 5,000, set by the developer

Appointing a broker and checking adverts

Form B is RERA's standard contract between a buyer and a broker. It records what the broker will do for you and the commission you will pay. Sign it before the broker starts work, so the terms are on paper.

Every property advert in Dubai must carry a Trakheesi advertising permit number, together with the brokerage's office registration number (ORN) and the broker's registration number (BRN). Printed and video adverts have carried a Madmoun QR code since 24 April 2023. Scan it and the Dubai Land Department confirms whether the advert is valid.

The steps from offer to title deed

  1. Make your offer.Your broker puts the offer to the seller's side. Agree the price, the deposit, the transfer date and who pays the developer's NOC fee.
  2. Sign Form F.Form F is RERA's standard sale contract between buyer and seller, often called the MOU. It records the price, the deposit and the date of transfer. By custom you pay a 10% deposit when you sign, and it is held until the transfer.
  3. Obtain the developer's NOC.The developer of the building issues a no-objection certificate confirming it does not object to the transfer. The fee is AED 500 to 5,000, set by the developer, and Form F records who pays it.
  4. Transfer at a registration trustee.Buyer and seller, or their attorneys, meet at a Dubai Land Department registration trustee office. The seller is paid, the fees are settled and the sale is registered.
  5. Collect your title deed.The title deed is issued in your name.

What happens on transfer day

The transfer is the moment ownership passes. It takes place at a Dubai Land Department registration trustee office, an approved office that handles the registration on DLD's behalf.

  • The seller receives the balance of the price
  • You pay the 4% transfer fee and the AED 520 title deed fees to the Dubai Land Department
  • You pay the trustee's fee: AED 4,000 plus VAT, or AED 2,000 plus VAT when the price is under AED 500,000
  • The sale is registered and the title deed is issued in your name

Bring your passport. If an attorney attends for you, they bring the original power of attorney in the form the Dubai Land Department accepts.

Buying off-plan instead

A home bought from a developer before the building is complete follows a different route. You reserve, sign the developer's sale agreement, pay in instalments into the project's escrow account, and register the purchase on the Oqood system for 4% of the price plus AED 20. There is no Form F with a private seller and no developer NOC on a first sale.

Buying off-plan in Dubai covers the whole process, and off-plan or ready helps you choose between the two.

Questions buyers ask

Can a foreigner buy property in Dubai?

Yes. Buyers of any nationality can own freehold property in Dubai's designated freehold areas, which were first listed in Regulation No. 3 of 2006 and include Dubai Marina, Downtown Dubai, Palm Jumeirah and Dubai Hills Estate. A UAE residence visa is not required.

What is Form F?

Form F is RERA's standard sale contract between a buyer and a seller, often called the MOU. It records the price, the deposit, the transfer date and who pays the developer's NOC fee.

How much deposit do I pay when I sign Form F?

By custom, 10% of the price. It is market practice rather than a legal requirement, and the deposit is held until the transfer.

What is Form B?

Form B is RERA's standard contract between a buyer and a broker. It sets out what the broker does for you and the commission you pay.

How much are the fees when buying property in Dubai?

About 7% of the price on a ready home: the 4% DLD transfer fee, AED 520 in title deed fees, the registration trustee fee of AED 4,000 plus VAT, the customary 2% agency fee plus VAT, and the developer's NOC fee of AED 500 to 5,000.

Where does the transfer take place?

At a Dubai Land Department registration trustee office. Buyer and seller, or their attorneys, meet there. The seller is paid, the fees are settled, the sale is registered and the title deed is issued in the buyer's name.

Do I need to be in Dubai to buy?

No. A non-resident buyer identifies themselves with a valid passport at the registration trustee, and an attorney can sign under a power of attorney that has been notarised, legalised, attested, translated into Arabic and issued within the last two years.

How do I check that a property advert is genuine?

Look for the Trakheesi permit number and the brokerage's ORN and broker's BRN. Printed and video adverts carry a Madmoun QR code. Scan it to confirm the advert with the Dubai Land Department.

The cost of buying property in Dubai, fee by fee

What the Dubai Land Department charges, what the broker and developer charge, who pays each one, and a worked example at AED 2,000,000.

Costs · Updated 15 September 2026 · 8 sources

In short

  • On a ready home, fees add about 7% to the price. The largest is the Dubai Land Department transfer fee of 4%, which by custom the buyer pays in full.
  • Fixed DLD fees are AED 520 for the title deed and AED 4,000 plus VAT for the registration trustee, or AED 2,000 plus VAT below AED 500,000. The customary agency fee is 2% plus VAT, and the developer's NOC costs AED 500 to 5,000.
  • At AED 2,000,000 the fees total about AED 131,720, or 6.6% of the price.
  • Off-plan homes pay 4% plus AED 20 on Oqood.

Fees on a ready home

A ready home is one that is built and has a title deed. When you buy one from its owner, five fees apply. Three go to the Dubai Land Department or its registration trustee, one to your broker and one to the developer of the building.

Developer NOCAED 500 to 5,000The developer

VAT in the UAE is 5%. It is added to the trustee fee and the agency fee. The transfer fee and the title deed fees are shown without it.

The transfer fee is a percentage, so it scales with the price. The title deed and trustee fees are fixed, so they weigh more on a cheaper home. That is why the total sits a little under 7% at AED 2,000,000 and a little over it on a smaller purchase: at AED 1,000,000 the same five fees come to about AED 70,720, or 7.1%.

Who pays which fee

By custom the buyer pays the whole 4% transfer fee. The Dubai Land Department's own fee schedule splits it equally between buyer and seller, so a different split is possible if both sides agree to it in Form F, the sale contract.

  • Transfer fee: the buyer, by custom
  • Title deed and trustee fees: settled at the transfer; our calculator shows them against the buyer
  • Agency fee: agreed between you and your broker. 2% plus VAT is the customary rate, not a legal one
  • Developer NOC: whoever Form F says. Agree it before you sign

If you are selling, the same fees apply. By custom your buyer pays the transfer fee, and your own agency fee is agreed in writing before the home is listed.

The NOC matters to both sides. Under Dubai Law No. 6 of 2019 a home may not be sold while its service charges are unpaid, so a seller settles them before the developer issues the certificate. Agree in Form F who pays the NOC fee, and ask for the figure before you sign rather than at the transfer.

A worked example at AED 2,000,000

Take a ready apartment at AED 2,000,000. The price is above AED 500,000, so the higher trustee fee applies. The NOC is shown at the top of the developer's range.

Fees on an off-plan home

A home bought from the developer before completion is registered on the Dubai Land Department's Oqood system rather than transferred at a trustee. The fee is the same 4% of the price, plus AED 10 knowledge and AED 10 innovation fees. At AED 2,000,000 that is AED 80,000 plus AED 20.

  • No trustee or title deed fee at this stage. Our calculator shows only the Oqood fee and the AED 20 admin fees on an off-plan purchase. The title deed is issued once the project has its completion certificate
  • No developer NOC. There is no previous owner on a first sale from the developer
  • Developer administration fees. Some developers charge their own, and they vary. Ask for them in writing before you reserve

Costs once you own

Owning carries running costs that are not part of the purchase but belong in the same budget.

  • Service charges: an annual charge for the upkeep of shared areas in a managed building or community. The rate differs by building. Under Dubai Law No. 6 of 2019 it needs RERA's approval on a budget approved by a certified audit firm, and a home cannot be sold while its service charges are unpaid. See service charges in Dubai
  • Income tax: the UAE does not levy income tax on individuals. Rental income from property held in your own name is not subject to corporate tax when the activity does not require a licence, under Cabinet Decision No. 49 of 2023
  • VAT on letting: residential leases are generally exempt. The first supply of a new residential property within three years of completion is zero-rated
  • Moving and furnishing: there is no official figure. Budget for them alongside the fees

Ask for the current service charge on any home you consider, and ask before you make the offer rather than after.

Questions buyers ask

How much does it cost to buy property in Dubai?

About 7% of the price on a ready home. The 4% DLD transfer fee is the largest part. The rest is AED 520 in title deed fees, the trustee fee of AED 4,000 plus VAT, the customary 2% agency fee plus VAT and the developer's NOC of AED 500 to 5,000. At AED 2,000,000 the total is about AED 131,720.

Who pays the 4% DLD transfer fee?

The buyer, by custom. The Dubai Land Department's own schedule divides the fee equally between buyer and seller, so the two sides can agree a different split in Form F.

What are the DLD title deed fees?

AED 520 for an apartment or villa: AED 250 for the title deed, AED 250 for the unit, and AED 10 each in knowledge and innovation fees.

What is the registration trustee fee?

AED 4,000 plus VAT when the price is AED 500,000 or more, and AED 2,000 plus VAT below that. The trustee is the DLD-approved office where the transfer takes place.

Is the agency fee fixed by law?

No. 2% of the price plus VAT is the customary rate in Dubai. The commission is agreed between you and your broker.

What fees does an off-plan purchase carry?

4% of the price to register on Oqood, plus AED 10 knowledge and AED 10 innovation fees, and any administration charge the developer sets. There is no developer NOC on a first sale from the developer.

What tax do you pay on property you own in Dubai?

The UAE does not levy income tax on individuals. Rental income from property held in your own name is not subject to corporate tax when the activity does not require a licence, under Cabinet Decision No. 49 of 2023. Residential leases are generally exempt from VAT.

How to buy property in Dubai when you live abroad

What changes when you are not in the UAE: the documents, the power of attorney rules, moving the money, visas and tax.

Buying · Updated 16 September 2026 · 12 sources

In short

  • A non-resident can own freehold property in Dubai's designated areas, and a valid passport is all the identification the registration trustee needs.
  • If you cannot be in Dubai, an attorney can sign for you. A power of attorney issued abroad must be notarised there, legalised by the UAE embassy or consulate, attested by the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs, translated into Arabic and dated within the last two years.
  • DLD's fully digital sale runs on Emirates ID, UAE Pass and a UAE bank account, so non-residents complete at a trustee office.
  • Owning property can bring a two-year investor residence visa, or a Golden Visa from AED 2 million.

Can a non-resident buy in Dubai

Yes. Dubai Law No. 7 of 2006 lets non-UAE nationals hold freehold property without a time limit, or leasehold rights of up to 99 years, in the areas designated for it. Those areas were first listed in 2006 and have grown since; every community in our area guides is one of them. You do not need a residence visa, a local sponsor or a UAE company to buy.

This guide covers an individual buying in their own name. A company buying through its licence has its own document list at the trustee, and a foreign company needs more of it.

What identification you need

The Dubai Land Department registers sales through registration trustee offices. A UAE resident shows an Emirates ID. A non-resident foreigner shows a valid passport. That is the whole identification requirement at the trustee.

On a resale in a freehold area the trustee also needs the developer's electronic no-objection certificate. The seller applies for it through the Dubai REST app, and it confirms the seller has paid the developer what is due. The sale contract, Form F, sets out who pays the NOC fee.

Buying without flying in

You can give someone a power of attorney to sign for you. DLD Circular No. 29/R/2025, as summarised by Dubai law firms, sets the standard for a power of attorney issued outside the UAE. It must be:

  • Notarised by the competent authority in the country of issue
  • Legalised by the UAE embassy or consulate in that country
  • Attested by the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs
  • Produced as the original, not a copy or a scan
  • Translated in full into Arabic by a UAE-licensed translator, where it is in another language
  • Dated within the two years before it is used

A power of attorney notarised inside the UAE and entered in the electronic register generally stays valid unless it states an end date. Whichever route you use, the document must name the transaction. General wording is rejected at the trustee, so have it drafted for this purchase, naming the property if you already know it.

Choose the attorney with care. A lawyer, a relative in the UAE or the brokerage can act, and the power of attorney can be limited to signing the contract and the transfer, with no authority over the money.

Getting the money to Dubai

DLD takes its own fees by ePay, Dubai Pay, the Noqodi wallet or manager's cheque. The price itself is a matter between you and the seller. By market practice it is paid by manager's cheque drawn on a UAE bank, handed over at the trustee office on transfer day. Plan how the funds will reach a UAE bank weeks before the transfer, not days: your own account if a bank will open one for you, or a lawyer's client account.

DLD's Digital Sale service on the Dubai Now app completes a sale online, but it is built for residents: both sides need an Emirates ID, an active UAE Pass and a UAE bank account, and the home must be in a freehold area with one owner. Non-residents use a trustee office, in person or through an attorney.

Budget the fees before you send money. On a ready home they come to about 7% of the price: the 4% transfer fee, AED 520 in title deed fees, the trustee fee, the agency fee and the NOC. The buying costs calculator gives the exact sum.

Visas that property can bring

  • A two-year investor residence visa. The Dubai Land Department's service page lists it for a sole owner of property of any value, or a joint owner whose share is worth at least AED 400,000, with an investor fee of AED 10,212.50.
  • A Golden Visa. For property worth AED 2 million or more, in one home or several. DLD and GDRFA Dubai describe it as ten years and renewable. The Golden Visa through property sets out the conditions.

In April 2026 GDRFA Dubai and DLD agreed to move property residency services into GDRFA Dubai's system. Confirm the current channel and fees before you apply, because the pages that describe them are changing.

Tax when you own from abroad

The UAE has no income tax on individuals. Under Cabinet Decision No. 49 of 2023, income from property you hold in your own name is outside UAE corporate tax, whatever the amount, as long as the activity is not carried out, and need not be carried out, under a licence. Residential leases are generally exempt from VAT.

Your own country may tax rent and gains from Dubai property, and some countries tax worldwide income. Ask an adviser at home before you buy, not after.

Questions buyers ask

Can a foreigner who does not live in the UAE buy property in Dubai?

Yes. Any nationality can own freehold property in Dubai's designated freehold areas under Law No. 7 of 2006, and no residence visa is needed. A valid passport identifies a non-resident at the registration trustee.

Can I complete a Dubai purchase without visiting?

Yes, through an attorney holding a power of attorney. One issued abroad must be notarised there, legalised by the UAE embassy or consulate, attested by the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs, translated into Arabic and dated within the last two years.

Do I need a UAE bank account to buy?

Not for DLD's fees, which it accepts by ePay, Dubai Pay, Noqodi or manager's cheque. The price is paid by manager's cheque from a UAE bank by market practice, and DLD's digital sale service requires a UAE account, so most non-residents open one or use a lawyer's client account.

Can I use DLD's online sale service from abroad?

No. The Digital Sale service on the Dubai Now app needs an Emirates ID, an active UAE Pass and a UAE bank account from both parties, which non-residents do not have. Non-residents complete at a registration trustee office.

Does owning property in Dubai give me a residence visa?

It can. DLD lists a two-year investor residence visa for a sole owner of property of any value, or a joint owner with a share of at least AED 400,000, and a Golden Visa, which DLD and GDRFA Dubai describe as ten years, for property worth AED 2 million or more.

Will I pay UAE tax on rent from a Dubai property?

No personal income tax applies in the UAE, and rental income from property held in your own name is outside corporate tax when no licence is needed. Your country of residence may tax the income.

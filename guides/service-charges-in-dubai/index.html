Service charges in Dubai, explained for owners and buyers

What the annual charge pays for, who sets it, where to look it up before you buy, and what the law does when an owner stops paying.

Owning and letting · Updated 16 September 2026 · 7 sources

In short

  • A service charge is the yearly amount each owner pays to manage, operate, maintain and repair the shared parts of a building or community.
  • Dubai Law No. 6 of 2019 stops a management company charging it until RERA has approved a budget that a certified audit firm has already approved.
  • You can see the approved charge for any building on DLD's Service Charge Index, through Mollak or in the Dubai REST app.
  • Unpaid charges are a lien on the home. It cannot be sold until they are cleared.

What the charge pays for

Every apartment building and most villa communities in Dubai are jointly owned property: you own your unit, and all owners share the lobby, lifts, pool, gym, corridors, landscaping, security and the structure itself. Dubai Law No. 6 of 2019 defines service charges as the annual charges collected from owners to meet the cost of managing, operating, maintaining and repairing those shared parts.

The charge is set per square foot of your unit, so a larger home pays more. Buildings with more facilities cost more to run, and so do older buildings that need more repair. A reserve fund for future replacement, such as lifts and chillers, sits inside the budget.

The charge does not cover your own unit. Repairs inside the home, your DEWA account and, where there is district cooling, your unit's chiller consumption are separate costs.

What a budget contains

An approved budget is itemised, and the manager must show it to owners. The usual lines are:

  • Cleaning, security and concierge staff
  • Lifts, fire systems, pumps and air handling: maintenance contracts and repairs
  • Pool, gym and landscaping
  • Electricity and water for the shared areas
  • Building insurance
  • The management company's fee
  • The reserve fund contribution for future replacement of major plant

Ask which lines rose in the last budget and why. Insurance and maintenance contracts are common reasons for an increase.

Who sets the amount

Each jointly owned building or community has a management entity: an owners' association, the developer, or a management company. Article 27 of Law No. 6 of 2019 forbids that entity from charging owners until the Real Estate Regulatory Agency (RERA) has approved the amount. RERA will not approve a budget that a certified audit firm has not approved first.

RERA runs this through Mollak, its system for service charge budgets, invoices and the companies that manage buildings. Owners are represented by an owners committee formed under the same law, which reviews the budget and raises concerns with the manager and with RERA.

How to check a building before you buy

  1. Look up the approved rate.DLD's Service Charge Index lists the service charges RERA has approved for jointly owned properties. It is on the DLD website, through Mollak, and in the Dubai REST app. Search by the building or community name.
  2. Multiply by the unit's area.The index gives a rate per square foot. Multiply by the area on the title deed to get the yearly charge for the home you are looking at.
  3. Confirm the seller is paid up.A home cannot be disposed of while its service charges are unpaid. On a resale the developer's no-objection certificate confirms the dues are clear, and Form F records who pays the NOC fee.
  4. Ask about the reserve fund and recent increases.A low reserve can mean a large one-off levy later. Ask the manager for the last two approved budgets.

If the seller cannot show a service charge statement, ask the manager directly before you sign Form F. The lien attaches to the unit, not to the seller: the trustee will not transfer without the NOC, and the developer will not issue it while charges are unpaid.

Service charges and your return

The charge is an owner's cost, every year, whether the home is let or empty. Law No. 6 of 2019 defines it as a charge collected from owners, so a landlord pays it, not the tenant, unless a tenancy contract says otherwise. Take it off the rent before you judge a home's yield, and add it to your running costs alongside insurance and any management fee.

If you are comparing two homes at similar prices, the service charge can decide between them. A difference of a few dirhams per square foot on a large apartment adds up over the years you own it.

When an owner does not pay

  • The management entity holds a lien over the unit, and the unit cannot be sold until the charges are paid (Article 32)
  • After a written notice approved by RERA, the owner has 30 days to pay before the entity can enforce the claim through the execution judge at the Rental Disputes Center
  • The judge can order the unit sold at public auction, with the court and legal fees falling on the owner

If you believe a charge is wrong, raise it with the owners committee and through Mollak before you withhold payment. Non-payment does not pause the lien.

If you disagree with the budget

The audit and RERA approval exist to keep budgets honest, but they do not make every budget right for every owner. The owners committee is the first route. It can ask the manager for the detail behind any line, and it can take concerns to RERA through Mollak. Disputes over jointly owned property go to the Rental Disputes Center, which has handled them since Law No. 6 of 2019.

  • Ask the manager for the line-by-line budget and the audit firm's approval
  • Raise it through the owners committee, which can take it to RERA through Mollak
  • For a claim, file at the Rental Disputes Center

Questions buyers ask

What are service charges in Dubai?

The annual charges each owner pays to manage, operate, maintain and repair the shared parts of a jointly owned building or community, as defined in Dubai Law No. 6 of 2019.

Who approves service charges in Dubai?

RERA. Under Article 27 of Law No. 6 of 2019 a management entity may not charge owners until RERA has approved the amount, and RERA first requires a budget approved by a certified audit firm.

How do I find the service charge for a building in Dubai?

On the Dubai Land Department's Service Charge Index, available on the DLD website, through Mollak and in the Dubai REST app. It gives the approved rate per square foot, which you multiply by the unit's area.

Does the tenant or the owner pay service charges?

The owner. Law No. 6 of 2019 defines service charges as charges collected from owners. A tenancy contract can shift some costs, but the obligation to the building sits with the owner.

Can I sell a home with unpaid service charges?

No. Unpaid service charges are a lien on the unit under Law No. 6 of 2019, and the unit cannot be disposed of until they are paid. The developer's NOC on a resale confirms the dues are clear.

What happens if an owner refuses to pay service charges?

After a RERA-approved written notice and 30 days to pay, the management entity can enforce through the execution judge at the Rental Disputes Center, who may order the unit sold at auction with the costs on the owner.

Can service charges go up?

Yes, but only through a new budget approved by a certified audit firm and then by RERA. Ask for the last two approved budgets to see the trend before you buy.
